⚡ Statistical Forecasting of Energy Demand and Grid Stability in Chile's SEN

An end-to-end R project -- ARIMA/SARIMAX, STL decomposition, state space models (ETS), and GARCH -- on the tidyverts/fable ecosystem

Code and method Full README

This page shows the project's results. The methodology, the design decisions and the limitations are documented in the repository's README.

Real hourly data and day-ahead machine learning (2018-2025)

70,128 real hours of the SEN from the Coordinador's public hourly-generation file, with Santiago temperature and holidays. Every input is at least 48 hours old; LightGBM is trained on 2018-2023, tuned on 2024 and tested on 2025, which it never saw.

Classical statistical models on real demand

ARIMA + Fourier, TBATS, SARIMAX, ETS and GARCH now run on the same real hourly demand (2024-2025), with solar and wind reconstructed from real weather and anchored to each day's real energy from the daily reports. Until this round only the monthly energy of the series was real. The headline changed: on the 14-day holdout, 18-31 December 2025, the same hour of the previous day scores 5.81% MAPE, against 7.01% for TBATS and 7.39% for ARIMA + Fourier. On the simulated series both beat it (4.28% and 4.54% against 6.73%). The holdout is Christmas and New Year, and in the hourly rolling-origin validation the worst window (8.57%) is the one containing Fiestas Patrias: none of these models knows a day is a holiday, while the LightGBM model above, which does, scores 2.48% over all of 2025. ETS on daily demand is the classical model that survives real data (1.91% against 2.78% for the weekly naive forecast in cross-validation). The GARCH estimate on wind ramps is not robust: anchoring wind to daily instead of monthly real energy moves it from α 0.010 / β 0.989 to α 0.363 / β 0.573.
